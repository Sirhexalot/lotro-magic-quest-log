Hello Damien,

I am the author of Magic Quest Log, a LOTRO plugin (quest and deed atlas):
https://github.com/Sirhexalot/lotro-magic-quest-log

The plugin itself needs nothing but its own files. For one optional feature I
would like to ship a small helper tool that reads the quest journal of the
running client, so a player can tell the plugin which quests they finished before
installing it. Reading the journal is only possible from memory, and for that the
tool uses delta-lotro-memory (6.8.1, with delta-lotro-dat-utils, delta-lotro-core,
delta-common, delta-json, slf4j-api and JNA, as declared in its POM), which I took
from my local LOTRO Companion installation.

It only reads: the character summary and the keys of m_completedQuestRecords and
m_currentQuestRecords. Nothing is written to the client, and no inventory, chat,
account or session data is touched. The result is a small Lua file that the plugin
reads.

To be clear about the question: I am not asking for code changes, and I do not
want to fork anything. I would like to know whether I may redistribute those jars
inside my download, and under which terms.

I am asking because I want to do this properly: LOTRO Companion's distribution is
Apache-2.0 and its NOTICE names you as the author of the delta-* libraries and
points to GitHub, but the repositories themselves carry no licence file, so I
cannot tell whether redistribution is intended.

If Apache-2.0 is the answer, I would bundle the jars unchanged, keep your
copyright notices, and include the Apache licence and a NOTICE that credits you
and states which jars are included. If you would rather I did not bundle them, I
will keep doing what I do today: the tool stays out of the download and the plugin
page simply points players to LOTRO Companion. That works, it is just more work
for the player, so I thought it worth one email.

If a different arrangement suits you better — a link to a place you already
publish, or keeping it export-only — that is fine with me too. Just tell me which
you prefer.

Thank you for LOTRO Companion itself; my whole catalog is built from the data it
extracts, and it has saved me an enormous amount of work.
